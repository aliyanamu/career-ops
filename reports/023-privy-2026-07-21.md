   **The gas sponsorship research and its correction trail:**
   - Hana's original belief, held during the integration: gas sponsorship requires smart wallets, smart wallets are a client-side user login feature, therefore her API-created hot wallets could not be sponsored.
   - First correction (wrong): the docs show a server-side path via `privy.wallets().create()` → `createViemAccount()` → `toKernelSmartAccount()` → `createSmartAccountClient()`. This is real but it is the **legacy** bring-your-own-paymaster approach, requiring an external vendor such as Pimlico.
   - Hana pushed back with dashboard screenshots showing the smart wallets toggle described purely in end-user login terms. That pushback was correct and forced a second round of verification.
   - **Verified truth:** Privy launched **native gas sponsorship on 24 Sep 2025** (`docs.privy.io/wallets/gas-and-asset-management/gas/setup`, plus the launch blog). For a server wallet it is a single `sponsor: true` parameter on `privy.wallets().ethereum().sendTransaction(walletId, {...})`. No smart account, no external paymaster. Server-side is the **default**; client-side sponsorship ships disabled. Requires TEE execution. EVM + SVM only.
   - Hana's integration began ~Apr 2025, so her model was correct when formed and the feature landed ~5 months in. **She ran manual gas top-ups for roughly 10 months against a solved problem.**

   The drafted entry framed this as "Privy shipped a fix and an existing paying customer never learned it existed." **That framing was later invalidated**: Nespay's actual reason for not using sponsorship was cost, not discoverability. One genuine open inference remains: no doc sentence states plainly that server-created wallets are sponsorship-eligible; it is inferred from the Node SDK example plus the dashboard toggle text.

   **Third correction, after Hana flagged that Kernel account support looked new to her.** An earlier draft called `gas/ethereum` a "legacy" page that the launch post "superseded." That claim required dating the page, which could not be verified, and if the server-side Kernel recipe were in fact recently added the claim would have been wrong. Verified instead, and the finding is now stated in a form checkable today: `gas/ethereum` opens "Privy makes it easy to create smart wallets for your users to sponsor gas fees," covers only the external-paymaster path, and **contains no link to native sponsorship and no deprecation notice**; `gas/setup` covers native sponsorship and **never mentions smart wallets or Kernel**. Two live pages answering the same question, neither referencing the other. Privy's own launch-post wording ("a major step forward from our earlier smart wallet–based approach") is quoted rather than paraphrased as a deprecation claim.

   **Standing rule that emerged from this:** no claim sent to a company may depend on a fact that cannot be re-verified against live docs on the day it is sent.
