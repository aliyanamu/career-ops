### Gaps and mitigation

**1. "Shipping AI-powered features in production" — the one blocking must-have.**
This is the single requirement that separates a strong application from a weak one, and `cv.md` does not support it. Everything else on the must-have list is a direct hit.

- Hard blocker? Borderline. They are an AI-native company hiring specifically for AI products, so it is likely a real screen and not decoration.
- **Question for Hana before applying:** what actually powered the ConnectX natural-language people search — embeddings/vector search, an LLM, or classic keyword/relevance ranking (Elasticsearch is on the skills list)? If it was semantic search, this gap shrinks a lot and the score moves toward 4.3. I will not guess, and it should not go in a cover letter until confirmed.
- Adjacent framing that is honest regardless of the answer: the JD's hard parts are async orchestration, retries, third-party API failure modes, and real-time pipelines. The Privy work is exactly that — a hand-written REST client with idempotency keys, error unwrapping, and a production signing bug traced to a provider's undocumented behaviour. LLM orchestration is the same problem shape with a different vendor.

**2. LLM / RAG / agentic and voice pipelines — nice-to-haves, both absent.**
- Not blockers on their own. Mitigation is a small, real artifact rather than a claim: a working RAG or agent-orchestration project with a public repo would cover both this and gap 1. That is a build, not a sentence — worth deciding on before applying, not after.
- Do not claim to be "learning" or "ramping on" LLM tooling unless that is actually true.

**3. No BFSI domain experience.**
- Weak gap. Nespay is B2B payments with KYC compliance (Sumsub, Kredibel), which is the nearest neighbour to regulated financial workflows. Enough to speak credibly about compliance constraints, audit trails, and why enterprise finance customers move slowly.
