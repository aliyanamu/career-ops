### Gaps

1. **Developer technologies (SDK / CLI / library + docs).** The single clearest gap — it is one of four listed responsibilities.
   - Hard blocker? No, it sits under Nice to Have, and the Must Have list is fully satisfied.
   - Adjacent: the Go indexer is effectively internal platform tooling the rest of the team builds on, and its webhook contract is a consumed interface. That is the same muscle, just unpublished.
   - Mitigation: frame the indexer as internal developer infrastructure in the cover letter. If she wants a stronger card, a small weekend TS SDK wrapping Somnia RPC (typed client + one CLI command + README) would close this gap outright and doubles as a "watch the ecosystem, spot gaps, build it" demonstration — which is literally a listed responsibility.

2. **Solidity / EVM contract authoring.** She integrates on-chain systems rather than writing the contracts.
   - Nice-to-have, not a blocker. Be honest: "I integrate against contracts and RPC, I haven't shipped production Solidity."
   - Adjacent: DeFi hackathon placement implies contract-adjacent work — worth checking what she actually wrote there before claiming or disclaiming.

3. **Design-led UI polish.** JD asks for "polished product UIs" as a plus and says the person should care as much about product as code.
   - Mitigation: lead with product reasoning, not visual craft — the CG Voucher Generator story (replacing hand-tracked Excel sheets with status visibility) is a product-thinking proof point, not just a build.

4. **AI tooling as a multiplier** — JD names it explicitly. Not a gap; it is an unclaimed strength. Her compound-engineering AI workflow belongs in the cover letter.

**Net:** Every Must Have is covered, most of them with direct evidence. The gaps are all in the Nice to Have column, and the biggest one (dev tooling) is closable in a weekend.

## C) Level and Strategy

**Detected level:** No YoE stated. The scope (own full lifecycle, self-directed, propose your own work) reads senior-ish, but the absence of a "Senior" title or a years bar means the band is open. At 6.5 yrs with end-to-end ownership at Nespay, she is squarely in range — per the seniority policy this is a match, not a stretch.

**Sell senior without lying:**
- Lead with breadth *in one company*: at Nespay she set up the architecture, the AWS auth and CI/CD, the NestJS backend, the React/Next dashboard, and works on the Go indexer. That is the exact "back-end through UI, plus internal platform" shape this JD describes.
- Use the reliability vocabulary they use: outage recovery, gap backfilling, dedup, pruning. It signals steady-state maturity, which is the second half of their "turn the dial" requirement.
- Ownership evidence over title: "Led development of the Backoffice Dashboard and backend server" and "developed core application architecture" are ownership claims that stand on their own.
- Do not claim to have started the indexer. She contributed to and operates it. The honest version is still strong.

**If they downlevel:** Accept if base is fair, given the token component is the volatile part anyway (see Block D). Ask for a written 6-month review with concrete scope criteria. In a foundation with this much surface area, scope growth is realistic if delivery is visible.
